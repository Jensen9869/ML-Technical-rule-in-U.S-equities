# Can Machine Learning Improve Technical-Rule Trade Selection?

A walk-forward study of machine-learning-assisted trade selection in U.S. equities.

[Open the research notebook](ML_technical.ipynb)

## Overview

This project investigates whether machine-learning models can improve trades generated by traditional technical-analysis rules.

Instead of predicting the next stock price directly, six deterministic rules first identify candidate trades. Every candidate is then evaluated with the same entry, exit, risk and transaction-cost assumptions. LightGBM and logistic regression estimate the probability that each trade will produce a positive risk-normalised return.

The central research question is:

> Given that a technical rule has generated a trade, can machine learning consistently distinguish stronger candidates from weaker ones after estimated transaction costs?

The current results provide limited evidence of useful market-regime timing, but weak evidence of stable stock-level selection ability.

## Data and Universe

The study uses daily U.S. equity data from January 2016 to October 2026.

| Item | Value |
|---|---:|
| Raw observations | 19,478,795 |
| Raw symbols | 16,025 |
| Common stocks and ADRs retained | 6,977 |
| Trading days | 2,714 |
| Simulated candidate trades | 141,891 |
| Out-of-sample trades | 103,097 |
| Walk-forward test period | 2019-2026 |
| Higher-confidence coverage period | October 2024 onward |

The daily tradable universe applies the following filters:

- Common stocks and ADRs only
- Closing price of at least USD 5
- Twenty-day median dollar volume of at least USD 2 million
- Among the 1,500 most liquid eligible securities
- Sufficient history to calculate the required indicators

The source database is accessed through a local `N_DB` module. Raw market data and database credentials are not included in this repository.

## Candidate Trading Rules

All signals are evaluated using information available at the close of day \(t\). Trades enter at the next available market open.

| Rule | Signal condition | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Breakout | Close exceeds the previous 60-day high and volume is greater than 1.5 times its 20-day average | Looks for price strength confirmed by unusually high volume |
| Pullback | Close is above MA50, MA20 is above MA50, price is within one ATR of MA20 and RSI(2) is below 10 | Looks for a sharp short-term decline within an established uptrend |
| Reversal | Low falls below the previous 20-day low, close recovers above that level and volume is above its 20-day average | Looks for a failed breakdown and possible seller exhaustion |
| Golden cross | MA50 crosses above MA200 | Looks for the start of a potential long-term bullish trend |
| KD cross | Stochastic %K crosses above %D while %K is below 30 | Looks for improving momentum from a weak-price region |
| MACD cross | MACD crosses above its signal line while MACD remains below zero | Looks for an early bullish momentum shift |

Only one rule is assigned to a stock on a given day. Further signals for the same stock are suppressed during the following 20-trading-day holding window.

## Trade Simulation and Target

Every candidate uses the same pre-defined trade simulation:

- Entry at the next trading day's open
- Stop loss at two ATR below entry
- Profit target at three ATR above entry
- Maximum holding period of 20 trading days
- Conservative stop-first assumption if both barriers are touched within the same daily bar
- Liquidity-tiered transaction costs charged on entry and exit

Each trade is measured in units of initial risk:

```text
R = net return / initial stop distance
```

The binary model target is `1` when net R is positive and `0` otherwise. The continuous R value is retained for economic evaluation.

## Features

The final model uses 15 features covering:

- Trend and moving-average distance
- Trend strength
- Position within recent price ranges
- Volume activity
- Sector-relative strength
- Volatility and volatility compression
- Liquidity
- Market return and market volatility
- Signal type

Stock-level features are expressed in ATR units or transformed into daily cross-sectional percentile ranks where appropriate.

## Models

Two classifiers are compared:

- **LightGBM:** a regularised nonlinear tree model
- **Logistic regression:** a simpler, interpretable baseline

The baseline is important because a complex model should only be preferred when it produces a clear and stable out-of-sample improvement.

## Walk-Forward Validation

The experiment uses expanding yearly walk-forward validation. For each test year:

1. Only earlier trades are used for training.
2. The latest available training year is used for LightGBM early stopping.
3. A 21-trading-day embargo separates training and testing.
4. Predictions are generated only for the unseen test year.

The embargo prevents a trade opened near the end of the training period from using an outcome that overlaps the test period.

## Main Results

### Classification performance

| Evaluation period | Trades | LightGBM AUC | Logistic AUC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| All out-of-sample years, 2019-2026 | 103,097 | 0.512 | 0.530 |
| Post-October 2024 higher-confidence period | 26,787 | 0.536 | 0.511 |
| Post-October 2024 without market features | 26,787 | 0.492 | 0.496 |

### Key findings

- Overall discrimination is weak and varies substantially by year.
- Logistic regression outperforms LightGBM across the complete out-of-sample period.
- Predicted probability deciles are not monotonic; the highest-probability decile has negative average R in the pooled test set.
- In the 2026 permutation test, `mkt_vol_20` and `mkt_ret_20` account for nearly all measured LightGBM importance.
- Removing the two market-regime features reduces recent performance to approximately random classification.
- Reversal, KD and MACD signals show positive ranking lift in selected tests, but those effects require further independent validation.

## Interpretation

The experiment does not provide strong evidence that LightGBM consistently identifies better individual stocks from technical signals.

Most measurable performance appears to come from recognising favourable or unfavourable market environments. That may be useful as a separate regime filter, but it is different from demonstrating stock-specific predictive ability.

The results are therefore exploratory and do not justify deploying the current model as a live trading strategy.

## Limitations

1. **Survivorship bias:** Data before October 2024 may not contain complete histories for securities that later delisted. The later sample has improved coverage but is not assumed to be fully bias-free without independent verification.
2. **Delisting treatment:** A disappearing security is closed at its final available price, which may overstate the amount shareholders could ultimately realise.
3. **Historical classifications:** Security type and sector use the latest available reference record rather than a fully point-in-time classification database.
4. **Overlapping observations:** Trades overlap and share exposure to common market events, so ordinary Welch t-statistics may overstate significance.
5. **Multiple testing:** Several rules, models, periods and selection methods are compared, creating data-mining risk.
6. **Estimated costs:** The cost model does not fully capture bid-ask spreads, slippage, market impact or order size.
7. **No portfolio constraints:** Trade-level R does not account for limited capital, simultaneous positions, position sizing or portfolio drawdowns.
8. **Probability calibration:** Predicted probabilities are not formally calibrated.
9. **Partial final year:** The 2026 test period ends in October and is not directly comparable with complete calendar years.

## Future Work

Future work could:

- Use fully point-in-time, survivorship-bias-free market data
- Separate market-regime prediction from cross-sectional stock ranking
- Calibrate predicted probabilities
- Apply clustered or bootstrap-based statistical inference
- Correct for multiple hypothesis testing
- Add portfolio construction, capital limits and position sizing
- Test the final specification on a completely untouched period
- Incorporate richer information such as historical sector classifications, financial metrics, company reports and news sentiment
